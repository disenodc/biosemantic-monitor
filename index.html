BioSemantic Monitor

A live, multi-source platform for exploring global biodiversity through Linked Open Data, Knowledge Graphs, and Visual Analytics. Connecting species occurrences with taxonomy, ecological interactions, literature, and conservation status in real time.

● 4 LIVE Sources ● 4 DEMO Sources 🗺️ 250 Countries 🧬 Semantic Enrichment 📊 Visual Analytics

The Project

The BioSemantic Monitor is an interactive web application designed to bridge the gap between raw biodiversity data and actionable ecological knowledge. By aggregating occurrence records from global repositories such as GBIF, and enriching them with taxonomic authority from the Catalogue of Life, biotic interactions from GloBI, and scientific literature treatments from Plazi, the platform builds a living semantic layer over the planet's biological data.

Every species record is not just a point on a map — it is a node in a vast, interconnected knowledge graph that reveals patterns of distribution, ecological roles, conservation threats, and evolutionary relationships. The system continuously monitors new data additions (Live Auto-Refresh), allows geographic and temporal filtering, and supports SPARQL-style semantic queries for advanced research workflows.

Core Subject: Visual Analytics for Linked Biodiversity Data

The conceptual foundation of this project is rooted in Visual Analytics applied to Linked Open Data (LOD) in the Life Sciences. Modern biodiversity informatics generates petabytes of occurrence records, genomic sequences, ecological traits, and taxonomic treatments — but these data live in isolated silos with heterogeneous schemas. The challenge is no longer collecting data, but making it semantically interoperable and visually explorable.

This platform implements the FAIR principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) by:

🕸️

Knowledge Graphs

Species, countries, institutions, and traits are modeled as RDF nodes connected by typed edges (foundIn, hasStatus, interactsWith). The D3.js force-directed graph renders these semantic relationships dynamically, allowing researchers to navigate from a single occurrence to its full ecological context.

🔗

Linked Open Data

Each taxon is anchored to stable identifiers (COL taxon IDs, GBIF keys) enabling cross-referencing between datasets. The RDF panel exposes Dublin Core and Darwin Core triples ready for SPARQL endpoints and semantic reasoning.

🌊

Ocean Governance & Marine Sciences

The architecture extends naturally to marine ecosystems, integrating data from oceanographic repositories. This aligns with research agendas in visual analytics for ocean governance, where decision-makers need real-time dashboards of species distribution, invasive species, and conservation hotspots.

🤖

AI-Driven Enrichment

Semantic enrichment pipelines combine API-based retrieval with heuristic classification to infer missing traits, detect endangered species, and suggest ecological interactions — laying the groundwork for future machine learning models over biodiversity graphs.

🌍

Country-Centric Exploration

The platform supports hierarchical geographic navigation (World → Continent → Country) with auto-generated subgraphs that reveal each nation's biodiversity portfolio, endemic species, and institutional contributors — essential for national biodiversity strategies and CBD reporting.

⚡

Real-Time Monitoring

The Live Auto-Refresh system monitors GBIF's occurrence stream and pushes new records into the visualization pipeline without user intervention, transforming the tool from a static explorer into a continuous biodiversity observatory.

Acknowledgments & Data Providers

This project is made possible by the open APIs and datasets provided by the global biodiversity informatics community:

🌐

GBIF.org

The Global Biodiversity Information Facility provides the backbone of occurrence records — over 2.5 billion indexed observations freely accessible through their public API.

📚

Catalogue of Life

The taxonomic backbone, delivering accepted names, classifications, and synonyms from hundreds of specialist databases unified in the COL XR checklist.

🔗

Global Biotic Interactions (GloBI)

The largest open repository of species interactions — predation, pollination, parasitism, symbiosis — powering the ecological edges of the knowledge graph.

📜

Plazi TreatmentBank

Semantically-structured taxonomic treatments extracted from the scientific literature, bringing centuries of taxonomic knowledge into the digital graph.
